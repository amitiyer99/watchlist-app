How this page works — and why it has no stop losses

The screen picks this cohort itself from your existing data: ROE ≥ 18%, D/E ≤ 0.5, promoter ≥ 50%, 5-year EPS CAGR ≥ 18%, market cap ₹500–15,000 Cr, liquid enough to trade — ranked by MBF score, top 20. The first time a name qualifies its fundamentals are snapshot, and that snapshot is the thesis, so nothing needs writing. A thesis break is then measurable: ROE down 6pp from entry, debt appearing, promoters selling 4pp, growth decaying, or a quarter of shrinking sales.

There are deliberately no price stops, targets or trailing exits. A stock that multiplies 10x draws down 30–50% more than once; the exit rules that make Triggers work would take you out at about 1.5x every time. Price here is context, never a signal. This is a research cohort, not a portfolio — what you buy and how much is your decision.

The strategy behind this page (tap to expand)

The arithmetic

A 10x over 8 years is 33% CAGR, and it comes from earnings growth × re-rating. A company compounding EPS at 25% for 8 years grows earnings ~6x; if the P/E also expands 1.6× as the market notices, that is your 10x. Miss either leg and you get 3–4x.

Why a cohort, not a few picks

These portfolios follow a power law. Out of ~20 names held 7–10 years, typically 1–2 do 10x and carry the entire result, 3–5 do 2–4x, 8–10 go nowhere and 4–6 lose most of their value. You cannot know which in advance — hence 20 names, each small enough that a total loss is survivable.

The sell rule

Drift from the entry snapshot, never a price move: ROE falling 6pp below entry or under 12% outright, D/E rising 0.35 above entry, promoter holding down 4pp, 5-year EPS CAGR decaying 8pp, or a quarter of shrinking sales. A flag is a prompt to look, not an instruction to sell.

What this page cannot tell you

Whether the strategy works. Your outcome ledger measures 5- and 20-day horizons over about three months of history — it cannot evaluate a 5-year thesis, and no engineering fixes that. The quarterly snapshots in docs/compounder-log.jsonl exist so that in three years there is a real record rather than a recollection.

Note also that names dropping out of the screen are kept and marked, not deleted — a cohort you can quietly edit after the fact teaches you nothing.
