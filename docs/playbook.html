The four jobs

Every page does exactly one of these. Confusing a job for another is the most common way to lose money with a system like this.

Job 5 · a separate bucket, reviewed quarterly

Hold compounders for years

Everything above optimises a 20-day horizon and exits on price. This bucket does the opposite: positions held on a written thesis and sold only when that thesis breaks on the quarterly numbers — no stops, no targets, no trailing exits. The 21-EMA trail that makes the trading side work is exactly what would exit a 10x at 1.5x, so keep the two buckets and their money separate. A 10x over 8 years is 33%/yr, and it comes from 1–2 names out of ~20 — so hold enough positions to catch one, each small enough that a total loss is survivable.

Your daily routine — about five minutes

  1. Read the email first

    If it flags an exit on something you already hold, act on that before looking at anything new. Protecting an open position beats finding another one.

  2. Open Triggers, sort by Ride Score

    Ride Score estimates how far a move can run: momentum, base quality, room to the next resistance, and earnings fuel. Use it to order your reading, not as a guarantee.

  3. Run the four-point checklist below on the top names

    A name has to pass all four. Most days, nothing does — and doing nothing is the correct action on those days.

  4. Check the market regime

    If the page says bear market, halve your size or stand aside entirely. In bear conditions the median 20-day result across this system was −3.48% versus −0.39% in bull. Same setups, much worse odds.

The buy checklist — all four, or skip it

Is it fresh?
Skip anything marked stale or triggered more than about 7 days ago. Breakouts are worth taking while they're new; the action window is roughly five trading sessions.
Is the price still at the pivot?
Buy at or within ~2% of the pivot. This is the single most important number in the system. Measured across every signal: at pivot the median was −0.54%, at 5–10% above pivot −3.01%, and 10%+ above pivot −5.61% with just a 27% win rate. Chasing is what does the damage.
Does reward:risk justify the trade?
Prefer 2 or better. Be aware of a real limitation: a target is only trustworthy when it's a structural level (a genuine prior high). Where no prior high sits above the pivot, the system falls back to a volatility estimate and the R:R lands near 1.5 by construction — those are marked with an asterisk. On an estimated target, don't trust the number: take the trade only if you'll manage the exit on the 21-EMA trail rather than at that price.
Am I using the suggested position size?
The Size % column is set so a single stop-out is survivable. Overriding it upward is how a good system produces a bad outcome.

Everything in this checklist is a filter, not a ranking. Passing all four doesn't make a name a winner — it makes it worth risking a measured amount on.

When to get out — the best-tested part of the system

This came from 5,061 simulated trades over 8 years of data. Selling half at target and trailing the rest beat selling everything at target by roughly 66% in expected profit per trade, with no drop in win rate. Your email sends these as SELL_HALF and EMA21_TRAIL alerts.

The exit rules

  • Sell half at the target. Bank the win, remove the temptation to round-trip it.
  • Trail the other half on the 21-day EMA. Keep holding while price closes above it; sell when it closes below. This is what lets a good trade become a big one.
  • The stop is not negotiable. If it hits, you're out. Moving a stop down to avoid a loss is the single most expensive habit in trading.
  • Exit on a 200-DMA breakdown alert. When price loses its long-term trend line, the reason you bought is gone.

Read this before you trust any score

Three honest caveats

  • Most pages are currently behind the index. Only Triggers shows positive measured alpha. Treat the rest as idea sources, not buy lists.
  • The scores don't rank yet. Every page was rated FLAT, meaning a score of 90 did not reliably beat a score of 50. Use scores as a threshold (“above 65 is worth a look”), not as a ranking (“the 97 is better than the 82”).
  • The sample is small and one-sided. A few weeks of mostly rising market. Anything measured here could look different after a real correction, which is exactly why the weights only move when the evidence clears a significance test.

Where the measured edge does live is the discipline, not the picking: entering at the pivot instead of chasing, sizing properly, honouring the stop, and exiting with half-plus-trail. Those four are worth more than any screener on this site.

A sensible next month

That produces clean data on the trades you'd actually make, which is what the learning loop needs to tune itself on something better than everything the screener emits.
